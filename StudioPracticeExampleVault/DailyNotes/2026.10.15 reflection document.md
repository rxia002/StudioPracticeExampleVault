1. **Inspiration**
I am interested in the repetition of experience and its relationship with the certainty.

When I experience the same thing many times, it becomes familiar. Once it is familiar, we can predict it, and being able to predict it makes us feel certain. Time and informations are also irreversible, once we have experienced something, we cannot forget it and return to the first time. So repetition seems to lead inevitably to certainty.

My start point is a personal experience, During a video call with my mum, I noticed that some things at home were not where I remembered them. One of the most familiar place in my life suddenly  felt strange. I don't know why. Maybe I had remembered it wrong. Maybe each time I recalled my home, I simplified it a little. Or maybe things had actually been moved. All of these possibilities existed at the same time, and I could not tell which one was true.

This made me start to question whether certainty is really worth pursuing. In comparison, this state of uncertainty, even of slight discomfort, is more interesting to me. Therefore, I want to bring the audience into this uncomfortable state, so that they begin to **question their memories**.

2. **Reflection and Tutorial Feedback**
**Concept:** 
The tutor asked whether "several versions existing at the same time" meant "splitting realities". This question made me realized that my idea is less about "parallel realities" and more about "memory distortion", which mean that **the gap between what I remember and what is actually there**. When I noticed that things at home were not where I remembered them, I couldn't tell whether my memory was wrong, whether I had simplified it through repeated recall, or whether things had really bean moved. What interests me is **this moment of not being able to tell**.

**Form:**
Passive: There are something changes subtly over time and the audience only notices when they come back
Active: The audience is asked to remember something and is then tested

**My reflection:**
I want to make an audio-visual cooperation work. For example, telling the audience "this is A" while showing B. I want to explore the audience's feeling about how sound or narration could slowly drift away from the image.

And since my work mainly about video and sound, I also need to think about time: how long the loops is, whether people will stay long enough to notice changes, and whether the work should be something people pass by or sit down with.
